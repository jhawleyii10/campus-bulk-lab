The basics

A short, practical primer — not medical advice.

PROTEIN

Protein supplies the building blocks (amino acids) your body uses to repair and build muscle tissue after training. Spreading protein across three to four meals a day is generally easier to hit consistently than trying to eat it all at once.

CALORIES

Calories are the total energy your body takes in from food. Eating somewhat more than you burn (a surplus) supports gaining weight and muscle over time; eating somewhat less (a deficit) supports losing body fat. Small, sustainable adjustments tend to work better long-term than extreme swings.

CARBS & FATS

Carbohydrates are a primary fuel source for training, especially higher-intensity lifting. Dietary fats support hormone production and overall health. Neither needs to be feared or eliminated — both fit into a balanced approach depending on your goals and preferences.

BULKING

A "bulk" generally means eating in a calorie surplus to support muscle growth. A gradual surplus with adequate protein and consistent training tends to add more muscle relative to fat than eating aggressively in excess.

CUTTING

A "cut" means eating in a calorie deficit to reduce body fat while trying to preserve as much muscle as possible — usually by keeping protein high and training intensity up even as total calories come down.

HYDRATION

Water supports nearly every process involved in training and recovery, including strength and endurance performance. Thirst, urine color, and body weight trends are simple everyday indicators most people can use to gauge hydration.

MEAL IDEAS

Simple, repeatable meals are usually more sustainable than elaborate ones: eggs and oats for breakfast, a protein source with rice and vegetables for lunch and dinner, and easy snacks like Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, or fruit in between. A plan you'll actually follow beats a "perfect" plan you won't.
